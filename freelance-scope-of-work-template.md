# Freelance scope of work template

Use this as a plain-language planning structure before a client approves a fixed-price or scoped project. Replace the placeholders with the exact project terms, then review the final agreement before work begins. This is planning guidance, not legal advice.

## Project

- **Client:** [name]
- **Project:** [project name]
- **Version/date:** [version and date]
- **Freelancer or studio:** [name]
- **Primary outcome:** [what the client should be able to do, receive, or decide]

## Deliverables

List outputs that can be reviewed and accepted. Avoid vague phrases such as “handle the website” without defining the result.

1. **[Deliverable 1]** — [what is included and the acceptance signal]
2. **[Deliverable 2]** — [what is included and the acceptance signal]
3. **[Deliverable 3]** — [what is included and the acceptance signal]

## Assumptions and client inputs

Write down the facts the price and schedule depend on.

- Client supplies: [copy, assets, access, decisions, or approvals]
- Client response window: [number of business days]
- Required tools or access: [systems, accounts, files, or environments]
- Working assumptions: [constraints, dependencies, or known unknowns]

If an assumption changes, explain whether the price, timeline, or deliverables must change too.

## Out of scope

Name work that is not included in the approved price. Examples include additional pages, new integrations, extra revision rounds, content production, ongoing support, third-party fees, or work for another audience or platform.

- [Exclusion 1]
- [Exclusion 2]
- [Exclusion 3]

Dates depend on the stated client inputs and response windows. Record a revised date when a dependency moves.

## Review and acceptance

- Included review rounds: [number]
- What counts as a revision: [changes that stay within the agreed deliverable]
- What counts as a defect: [failure to meet an agreed acceptance criterion]
- Acceptance method: [written approval, delivered file, published page, or other signal]
- Approval window: [number of business days]

Separate correcting an agreed deliverable from requesting a new direction or output.

## Price and payment

- **Project price:** [amount and currency]
- **Payment schedule:** [deposit, milestones, final payment]
- **Payment due dates:** [dates or triggers]
- **Expenses or third-party fees:** [included, excluded, or approval rule]

The price covers the deliverables, assumptions, review rounds, and timeline written above.

## Changes after approval

Requests that change a deliverable, revision limit, client input, acceptance criterion, or deadline are reviewed before the extra work begins.

For each requested change, record:

- What the client is asking to add or change
- Why it differs from the approved scope
- Added effort, coordination, risk, or rush impact
- Added price and any schedule impact
- Whether the original scope remains available as the alternative
- The written approval and date

## Approval

By approving this scope, the parties confirm that the deliverables, assumptions, exclusions, review process, timeline, price, payment schedule, and change process are clear enough to begin the project.

- **Client approval:** [name / date / written confirmation]
- **Freelancer or studio confirmation:** [name / date]

## Final pre-send check

- Does every deliverable describe a reviewable result?
- Are client inputs, assumptions, exclusions, and dependencies visible?
- Are revisions and acceptance separated from new work?
- Do price and payment match the defined scope?
- Does the change process require agreement before extra work begins?
