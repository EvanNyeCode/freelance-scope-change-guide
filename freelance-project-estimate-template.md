# Freelance project estimate template

Use this template to turn a rough project request into a clear estimate before you send a quote. An estimate explains the work and the assumptions behind the number; it is not a promise to absorb later scope changes for free.

This is planning guidance, not legal or accounting advice. Adapt it to your agreement, client, location, and project.

## What a useful estimate should make clear

- The outcome the client wants.
- The deliverables and what is explicitly excluded.
- The assumptions and client inputs the estimate depends on.
- The work phases or task groups being estimated.
- The effort, rate or pricing basis, and total estimate.
- The expected schedule and the conditions that could move it.
- Included revisions, acceptance criteria, payment terms, and the next approval step.

## How to estimate without hiding the uncertainty

1. Break the request into deliverables or phases that can be reviewed separately.
2. Estimate the work each phase requires using the pricing basis that fits the agreement.
3. List dependencies and uncertainty instead of burying them inside an unexplained buffer.
4. State the assumptions that would make the estimate change.
5. Give one clear total or a clearly labeled range, then explain what would move it.
6. Keep the approved estimate visible when a later request changes the scope, price, timing, or acceptance criteria.

## Estimate, quote, and change request are different

- An **estimate** explains expected effort or cost while assumptions may still need confirmation.
- A **quote** presents a client-facing price for a defined scope and terms.
- A **change request** records a later request that changes the approved scope, price, schedule, or acceptance criteria.

Do not silently turn a new request into free work. Compare it with the approved estimate or quote, describe the impact, and get written approval before starting the added work.

## Final review

- Can the client tell what outcome they are buying?
- Are deliverables, exclusions, assumptions, and dependencies visible?
- Can the total be traced to a pricing basis?
- Are schedule, revisions, acceptance, and payment clear?
- Does the document explain what happens when the request changes?
